# Email Rules

## Subject and preview are one unit

- Subject: 30 to 50 characters, curiosity or a clear benefit. Honest about the content. No spam triggers (ALL CAPS, "FREE!!!", stacked punctuation).
- Preview: expands or completes the thought. Never repeats the subject.
- Example: Subject "Your funnel is bleeding leads", Preview "Here's where, and how to fix it".
- Offer 2 alternative subjects for the key email.

## Opening line

Use "you" language and get to the point.
- Weak: "I want to share something with you today."
- Strong: "You're losing 60% of your leads between click and signup."

## Body arc

Story or problem, insight, solution, CTA.

- 2 to 3 short paragraphs of insight. Short sentences, white space, scannable on a phone.
- Write to one person. Conversational, like a message from someone who knows them.
- One primary CTA. It may appear 2 to 3 times but it is always the same action.
- P.S.: reinforce the CTA, add a secondary benefit, or a real deadline. Skip if it adds nothing.

## Length bands

| Band | Words | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Short | 150 to 300 | Re-engagement, reminders, promo nudges, simple newsletters |
| Standard | 300 to 500 | Most broadcasts, nurture emails, welcome emails |
| Long-form | 500 to 800 | Story-led sales emails, deep newsletters, launch anchor emails |

The user's choice beats these defaults.

## Templates by type

### Newsletter

```
Subject / Preview
Hook line (a sharp observation, a result, or a short story)
Main insight (the one thing worth their time)
Optional quick hits (2 to 3 one-line items, each with a takeaway)
CTA (reply, read, watch or click, one only)
Sign-off + P.S.
```

### Promo broadcast

```
Subject / Preview
Problem or moment (why now)
The offer in one plain sentence (outcome first)
Proof (result, story or testimonial, real only)
Objection handled
CTA (repeat once)
P.S. (deadline if real, or the strongest benefit)
```

### Welcome sequence (default 4 emails)

1. **Day 0:** deliver what was promised, set expectations (what they will get and how often), one easy first step.
2. **Day 1 or 2:** your story or why this matters, tied to their problem.
3. **Day 3 or 4:** the quickest win or most useful lesson.
4. **Day 6 or 7:** soft offer or next step, with proof.

### Nurture sequence (default 5 emails)

Each email: one lesson, one story or example, one small next step. Order them by the reader's journey from problem awareness to solution awareness. Only the last one or two make a direct ask.

### Launch or webinar sequence (default 5 emails)

1. **Announce:** what is happening and who it is for.
2. **Teach:** a useful lesson tied to the offer.
3. **Proof:** a story, result or testimonial.
4. **Reminder:** logistics and the value of showing up, or objection handling for a cart.
5. **Close:** last call, only with a real deadline.

For webinars, add a "starts today" reminder and a replay email if the user has a replay.

### Re-engagement (default 2 to 3 emails)

1. Short, human, no guilt. Ask one easy question or offer one useful thing.
2. A fresh reason to come back (new resource, result or offer).
3. Final "should I keep emailing you?" with a clear opt-out and a clear stay.

## Sequence spacing defaults

Use the user's spacing. If none is given, use daily for the first 2 emails of a welcome sequence, then every 2 to 3 days. Use a compressed schedule only for launches and webinars with real dates.

## CTA rules

- One action. Specific verb and outcome: "Book your free audit", not "Click here".
- Match friction to warmth. New subscribers: reply, read or watch. Engaged: download or register. Customers or hot leads: book or buy.
- Urgency only when real ("Doors close Friday at 5pm"). Never invent scarcity.

## Housekeeping

- Do not add unsubscribe or address blocks. The email platform adds them. Mention it only if the user asks.
- Do not promise results you cannot support. Use real proof or none.
- If the email touches income, health or financial claims, keep the language directional and flag any claim that needs substantiation.
