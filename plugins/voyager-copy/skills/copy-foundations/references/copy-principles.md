# Core Copy Principles

Applies to every deliverable in this plugin. Skill-specific rules (ad package, email blueprint, page blueprint) sit on top of these.

## 1. Hooks and first lines

The first line is the main conversion point. Assume the reader has zero brand awareness and will leave after one line unless it earns attention.

- Lead with a diagnosis, a contrarian take, a specific problem, or a clear outcome.
- Skip setup sentences ("Have you ever wondered...", "Today I want to share...", "Let me tell you a story...").
- Avoid question hooks. Use curiosity gaps sparingly, because both are overused.
- Test: if everything except line 1 were removed, would someone still engage?

| Hook type | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Contrarian | "You don't need more traffic. You need better messaging." |
| Clarity-based | "Your funnel isn't broken. Your awareness stage mapping is." |
| Pattern interrupt | "We cut our ad budget in half and doubled revenue." |
| Belief audit | "Most coaches think they need more leads. They need better qualification." |
| Relatable pain | "You're booking 2 calls a week when you need 10." |

## 2. Pain and audience language

Translate abstract pain into a concrete daily problem with a number, a scenario or a tool they touch every day. Mirror the customer's own words, not the marketer's.

| Abstract | Concrete |
|---|---|
| "Need more leads" | "You're booking 2 calls a week when you need 10 to hit your goal." |
| "Low conversions" | "Sales calls go well, then 7 of 10 prospects ghost after the proposal." |
| "Scaling issues" | "You can't take more clients without hiring, and you can't hire without more clients." |

Replace features with outcomes. Use industry jargon only if the audience uses it in daily conversation.

## 3. Funnel awareness

Identify the awareness stage before writing. Match message depth to it.

| Stage | Write | Do not write |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | A surface symptom made tangible | A solution pitch or mechanics |
| Problem-aware | The root cause, and why obvious fixes fail | A product pitch |
| Solution-aware | How your approach differs, briefly, with proof | A re-explanation of the problem |
| Product-aware | Objection handling, proof, risk reversal | The problem again |
| Most aware | The offer plus one real reason to act now | More education |

Temperature shortcut: cold = unaware or problem-aware, warm = solution-aware, hot = product-aware or most aware.

## 4. Objections

Answer objections inside the copy before the reader raises them. Weave them through the piece, not only at the end.

- "No time": say how long it takes and that it runs without them.
- "Tried it before": acknowledge it and say what is different.
- "Too expensive": frame it against what they already spend or lose.
- "Will it work for me": use a scenario that matches their exact situation.

## 5. Authority

Show understanding by diagnosing the problem and explaining cause and effect. Credentials are the weakest signal. Order of strength: explanation, case study, data, credentials.

## 6. One message, one CTA

One core idea per asset. No stacked benefits, no competing CTAs. If the core message cannot be said in one sentence, simplify until it can.

## 7. CTA friction by temperature

| Temperature | Goal | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Cold | Start a conversation or a small step | Comment, reply, DM, soft click |
| Warm | Move to the next stage | Download, watch, register |
| Hot | Convert | Book, start, buy |

Friction ladder, lowest to highest: comment, DM, click to view, click to landing page, form fill, calendar booking, purchase. Match friction to awareness.

## 8. Conversion elements

- **CTA:** one explicit action with an action verb. Never "Click here" or "Learn more" when something specific is possible.
- **Value proposition:** the outcome and why it matters to this person. Format: action, outcome, without the common obstacle.
- **Proof:** for warm and hot traffic. Results with context ("47% more leads in 30 days"), testimonials, case studies.
- **Urgency:** only when there is a real reason ("Doors close Friday"). Never invent scarcity. Forced urgency destroys trust.

## 9. Clarity

If they do not understand it in 3 seconds, they will not convert.

- Plain words, average 10 to 15 words per sentence, vary the rhythm.
- No buzzwords (revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-level, world-class).
- No filler ("in order to", "at the end of the day", "in today's competitive landscape").
- Read it aloud. If it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.

## 10. Brand voice

Whatever brand voice, tone, vocabulary and banned words the user or project provides beats the defaults in this plugin. Where none is provided, default to plain, direct and conversational, and say so in the assumptions.

## 11. Common failures

- Generic openers and slow setup.
- Abstract pain ("struggling to scale").
- Feature talk instead of outcomes.
- Selling to cold traffic, or re-explaining the problem to hot traffic.
- Two or three CTAs in one asset.
- Guaranteed results, ego-driven credentials, unsupported superlatives.
- Copy that could be sent by any brand in the category.
