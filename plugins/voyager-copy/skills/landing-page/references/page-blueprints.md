# Landing Page Blueprints

## Principles that apply to every page

**Conversion singularity.** One page, one goal, one action.
- Define the single action (book, download, register, buy) and remove every competing action.
- One CTA repeated 2 to 4 times with the same words and the same destination.
- No navigation menu (logo only), no footer link clusters, no social icons, no external links that leave the page, no stacked offers. Keep a privacy or terms link if the user needs one.
- Every section moves toward the CTA. Linear flow: headline, problem, solution, proof, CTA.

**Above-the-fold formula.** In 5 to 10 seconds the page answers: what is this, why should I care, what do I do next.
1. **Headline:** outcome-driven and specific, what they get and not what you do. Bad: "Marketing Solutions for Growth". Good: "Get 20+ Qualified Leads a Week Without Cold Outreach".
2. **Subheadline:** expands or de-risks. "No contracts. No ad spend required."
3. **CTA button:** specific verb. Not "Learn More" or "Get Started". Use "Book My Free Call" or "Download the Checklist".
4. **Trust line:** logos, testimonial count or rating. Real only.

**5-second clarity test.** Someone unfamiliar with the offer should be able to say what it is, who it is for and what to do. If not, rewrite.

**Problem-to-action structure.** Do not sell the solution, sell the next step. Cold traffic needs diagnosis, not a feature list.

**Length by temperature.** Cold: longer, more education. Warm: medium. Hot: short, offer and CTA.

**Message match.** The page headline must echo the promise of the ad or email that sent the visitor. A mismatch raises bounce rate and ad costs.

## Form friction ladder

| Level | Fields | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email only | Cold lead magnets |
| 2 | Name and email | Standard lead magnets |
| 3 | Name, email, phone | Call bookings, high-intent offers |
| 4 | Name, email, phone, 1 qualifier | Only when qualification is necessary |
| 5 | 5 or more fields | Checkouts and applications only |

Rules: cold traffic gets level 1 or 2, warm level 2 or 3, hot level 3 or 4. Collect anything else after opt-in (progressive profiling). Add a line under the form saying what happens next ("You'll get instant access to the guide") and a short privacy line ("We'll never spam you or share your info").

## Blueprint A: Opt-in (lead magnet)

Length: short to medium.

1. Above the fold: headline, subheadline, CTA button (opens or scrolls to the form), trust line, optional mockup note.
2. Problem recognition: the pain in their words (2 to 4 lines).
3. What is inside: 3 to 5 outcome bullets, not features.
4. Who it is for and who it is not for.
5. Proof: short testimonial or result.
6. Final CTA with the form and under-form text.

## Blueprint B: Webinar registration

Length: medium.

1. Above the fold: headline (the outcome of attending), subheadline with date, time and time zone, CTA "Save My Seat", trust line.
2. What you will learn: 3 to 5 takeaways.
3. Who it is for.
4. Host: short credibility, explained by results and not by titles.
5. Proof.
6. Objections (time, replay, cost).
7. Final CTA and form (level 2 or 3).

## Blueprint C: Book-a-call

Length: medium for cold, short for warm.

1. Above the fold: outcome headline, subheadline that frames the call as a fit check, CTA "Book My Free Call", trust line.
2. Problem recognition.
3. Why it exists (root cause, reframed as solvable).
4. What changes (the shift, not the product).
5. What happens on the call: 3 steps, and who it is for and not for.
6. Proof.
7. Objections and FAQ.
8. Final CTA with the booking widget and a short qualifying form if needed (level 3 or 4).

## Blueprint D: Sales page

Length: long. Cold traffic gets the full arc.

1. Above the fold: headline, subheadline, CTA, trust line.
2. Problem recognition.
3. Why it exists.
4. What changes.
5. The offer: what it is, what is included, how it works, who it is for.
6. Proof: case studies, testimonials, data.
7. Benefits: outcomes, not features.
8. Bonuses and value stack (only what the user supplied).
9. Price and payment options (only what the user supplied).
10. Guarantee (only if the user has one).
11. FAQ and objection handling.
12. Final CTA, repeated at least 3 times across the page.

Never invent a guarantee, bonus, price or result.

## Blueprint E: Thank-you page

Length: very short.

1. Confirmation headline: they are in, and what just happened.
2. What happens next: 2 or 3 steps with timing.
3. One next action: watch a video, add to calendar, join a group, or download. Choose one.
4. Optional reassurance: check spam folder, who to contact.
5. No new offers unless the user asks for one.

## Section patterns (reusable)

**Problem recognition:** state the pain with a number or scenario, in their words. "You're stuck at $10k a month because every client needs custom onboarding."

**Why it exists:** diagnose the root cause. "It's not that you need to work harder. You need a system that runs without you."

**What changes:** the shift, not the product. "What if every lead got a reply in 3 minutes, automatically?"

**Proof:** a specific result with context. "Sarah went from 2 calls a week to 15 without hiring." Use only what the user provided.

**Next step:** frame the CTA as "see if this fits" and not "buy now" for cold traffic.

## GoHighLevel build notes (only when the platform is GHL)

- Use the native form builder for faster load and enable autofill and autocomplete attributes.
- Mobile-first: single column, tap targets at least 44px, large readable text.
- One-click submit, no CAPTCHA unless spam is severe.
- Logo-only header, no menu, no footer links beyond privacy and terms.
- Match the button text and color across the page, and connect the form or calendar to the intended workflow and tag.
- Confirm the redirect to the thank-you page and that tracking fires on it.
- Test on mobile before publishing.
