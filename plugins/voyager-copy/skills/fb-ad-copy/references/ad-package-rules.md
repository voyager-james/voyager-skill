# Ad Package Rules

## Package format

Every ad ships as one package, never a single block of copy.

| Element | Count |
|---|---|
| Primary text | 1 |
| Headlines | 5 (variants to test against the primary text) |
| Description | 1 (20 to 30 words) |
| CTA | 1 (button label, matched to the goal and tone framework) |

## Primary text: the 6-beat template (default)

Hook, Relatable Pain, Why It Happens, What Changes, Realization, CTA.

```
[Hook: speaks to the audience's pain, problem, desire, outcome or solution]

[Relatable Pain: the situation or story, in their words]

[Why It Happens: the root cause]

[What Changes: the outcome, without a solution pitch]

[Realization: the aha moment that makes the next step obvious]

[CTA]
```

Rules:
- The first 125 characters carry the ad. Assume "See more" is not clicked. The first sentence must work alone.
- Mobile formatting: single-line paragraphs with a blank line between thoughts. No walls of text.
- The hook must lead with the audience's pain, problem, desire, outcome or solution. Use their own wording. Never a generic opener.
- Repeat the CTA 2 to 3 times (top, middle, end).
- Avoid emoji overload, ALL CAPS and excessive punctuation.
- Embed one audience qualifier naturally in the body (team size, revenue tier, stage, job title) so the wrong-fit person self-selects out. "Any business owner at any stage" qualifies no one.

Short example of the shape (illustrative, adapt to the real offer):

```
Your cost per lead isn't a Facebook problem. It's a messaging problem.

You've rewritten the headline four times and split-tested three images. The cost per lead still won't move, and it starts to feel like the platform is broken.

Here's what is usually happening: the promise in the ad and the headline on the landing page don't match. Facebook reads that gap as low relevance and charges you more for it.

Close the gap and the same budget starts buying more leads.

The ad was never the problem. The handoff was.

Want the breakdown? Comment "GUIDE" and I'll send it over.
```

## Tone frameworks (advanced, pick one per ad)

Each compresses Relatable Pain and Why It Happens into one Body beat. Rotate across a set.

| Framework | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern Interrupt | Calls out the paradox: achievement without the expected payoff | Audiences stuck but unable to name why |
| Aspirational | Validates success, then reframes what comes next as the real challenge | Proven achievers who hit a ceiling |
| Challenge-Led | Names the plateau, positions a system as the fix | Stagnant audiences working harder with no result |
| Social Proof | Reframes the audience as successful but overstretched | Audiences where trust is the first hurdle |

```
Pattern Interrupt
  Hook: state the contradiction flatly (what they achieved vs what is still broken)
  Body: why success created the problem, the real cause (system, not effort), the solution
  CTA:  curiosity-led, "See How It Works"

Aspirational
  Hook: acknowledge the achievement, introduce the "but"
  Body: what they built got bigger, not easier; more of them is required; the real gap is freedom or growth
  CTA:  invitation, "Book a Free [Session / Call / Demo]"

Challenge-Led
  Hook: name the stage every [audience] hits
  Body: things are in place but growth flatlined; working harder is not moving it; it needs a system
  CTA:  selective, "Apply for [X]"

Social Proof
  Hook: "The [clients / people / owners] we work with aren't struggling."
  Body: successful on paper but exhausted or bottlenecked; it would stall without them; the solution builds structure
  CTA:  self-qualifier, "See If You Qualify"
```

One framework per ad. Never use pressure CTAs ("Last Chance", "Before Spots Fill Up") with any framework.

## Headlines: 10 rules

Never fewer than 5. Run every headline through all 10 rules and the checklist.

1. **Talk to them, not about them.** Use "you" and "your".
   Bad: "The Business Grows. The Owner Never Gets Free." Good: "Your Business Is Growing. You Still Can't Take a Day Off."
2. **Name the audience** when the headline could apply to anyone.
   Good: "Business Owner: Stop Being the Bottleneck in Your Own Company."
3. **Use the prospect's words.** Cut jargon. If a word needs explaining, it does not belong.
4. **Pick the strongest verb.** "Survive" raises stakes over "keep running".
5. **Make abstractions concrete.** "Can't Take a Day Off" beats "Never Gets Free".
6. **Cut every word that is not earning its place.** It must land before "See more" on mobile.
7. **Raise the stakes.** A question they already know the answer to is forgettable. "Could Your Business Survive 2 Weeks Without You?"
8. **Drop dates and logistics** unless they are the whole point. The body holds the date.
9. **Do not lecture, show the cost.** Replace "even the ones that shouldn't" with "That's why you're exhausted."
10. **Sharpen every contrast.** Name the specific opposite. "You Built Your Business for Freedom. Now It Owns You."

### Headline checklist

- [ ] Uses "you" or "your"
- [ ] Only the target could read it and think it is for them (else name the audience)
- [ ] No word the prospect would have to think about
- [ ] No stronger verb available
- [ ] Abstract feelings replaced with a concrete image
- [ ] No cuttable word
- [ ] The question has real stakes
- [ ] A two-sentence headline names a cost and does not judge the reader
- [ ] Any contrast is specific and not vague

## Description rules (20 to 30 words)

1. Aligns with the primary text.
2. Names the specific offer (free guide, free session, product), never generic.
3. Reinforces the hook in one clear sentence without repeating it word for word.

Format: core claim from the primary text plus a specific offer reference.

| Framework | Example pattern |
|---|---|
| Pattern Interrupt | "Discover the exact framework that turns [problem] into [outcome]. Download free." |
| Aspirational | "For established [audience] ready to grow without working harder. Free access inside." |
| Challenge-Led | "Built for [audience] ready to move from [current state] to [desired state]. Get it free." |
| Social Proof | "The system successful but stretched [audience] use to [outcome] without being the bottleneck. Download free." |

Bad: "Learn more about our program."

## Audience qualification

Use body language to qualify the right buyer in and let the wrong buyer out. State a signal (stage, revenue, team size, job title, time in business, industry) and be precise about who it is for.

| Signal | Phrasing |
|---|---|
| Stage | "Established [owners / professionals]" |
| Revenue or team | "Already generating [level], but..." |
| Implicit exclusion | "This isn't for startups or beginners." |
| Readiness | "For [audience] ready to invest in the next level" |
| Specificity | "[Descriptor] with [detail] who [specific problem]" |

## CTA matching

| Temperature | CTA style |
|---|---|
| Cold | Comment or DM ("Comment GUIDE and I'll send it"), or a soft click |
| Warm | Link to a guide, training or registration |
| Retargeting | Direct: book, apply, start |

Match the button label to the framework (Aspirational: "Book a Free Session", Challenge-Led: "Apply for X", Social Proof: "See If You Qualify"). No high-pressure urgency, ever.
